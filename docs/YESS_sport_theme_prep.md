## Exec summary (for me)

One thesis, three questions, one proof.

**The thesis:** Grassroots and amateur sport has no data or operating infrastructure. That single gap explains why people drop off, why private money avoids it, and where the opportunity is.

**Why I can own this:** I played ~10 years, semi-pro reps, and I researched this problem to the ground for the LFF, including a primary-source interview with Tim Berggren, who built Denmark's LigaLogin (200k+ users). Most people in the room have an opinion. I have field time and primary research.

**How I deploy it:**
- **Build:** an operating system for small clubs / rec leagues (registration, dues, scheduling, comms). This is my LFF insight ported from spectators to participants.
- **Talk:** frame participation as a retention/churn funnel. That is my actual day job at Kilo, and nobody else will make that connection.

**The one line I say out loud when the theme comes up:**
> "The problem in amateur sport isn't motivation, it's infrastructure. There's no data, no tooling, no professional organizing layer, so it can't grow and money can't find its way in. Everyone builds another fitness app. Almost nobody builds the plumbing underneath grassroots sport."

## The three questions, answered

### 1. What keeps people from exercising? (team sport specifically)

Not motivation. The absence of a **professionalized organizing layer**.

A gym class has a fixed time and someone paid to run it. Amateur team sport has neither. It collapses onto whoever is willing to organize for free, and when that person burns out, the whole group dies. The constraint is reliability, not willpower. People want to play. The system that would let them play consistently doesn't exist.

### 2. Why so little private money in sport?

Money floods the top 1% (media rights, betting, elite clubs) and avoids grassroots. Three reasons, and they compound:

- Grassroots is fragmented into thousands of tiny orgs.
- Those orgs hold **no usable data**, so they're invisible to sponsors and investors.
- With no ops layer, there's no way to aggregate or scale them.

This is a vicious cycle: no data, no commercial value, no investment, no growth, which keeps them too small to justify data. It's the exact cycle I documented in the LFF work. I can defend every step of it.

### 3. Where's the opportunity?

The **missing infrastructure layer** for grassroots sport: identity, data, payments, retention. The "Stripe / Shopify for amateur sport organizations." Whoever builds that layer unlocks the money that currently has no way in.

Two flavors, deployed differently:
- **Consumer coordination** (find-a-game apps): crowded graveyard, weak payer, cold-start problem. Avoid.
- **B2B ops / data** (tooling for the org): defensible, clear payer, and it's where my edge lives. This is the lane.

## The Denmark proof (my credibility flex)

- LigaLogin, built by Denmark's Divisionsforeningen. One login across 25+ platforms (tickets, apps, merch, streams).
- 200,000+ users. One mid-size Superliga club generated ~2,000 new logins in a single evening around one cup broadcast.
- I interviewed Tim Berggren, who built it. That's primary-source research most people my age don't have.
- The transfer: same missing-infrastructure pattern applies to people *playing*, not just watching.

Use this as evidence the infrastructure gap is real and solvable, not as a ticketing pitch.
